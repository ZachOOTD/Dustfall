# Decisions

Numbered key calls — why + when. Don't re-explain in chat — link here.

When adding: append at the bottom with the next D-number. Don't renumber.

Each entry carries a `**friction-score:**` (0-5) reflecting how often the
decision will cause future rework or pain. Added in the gamedev-framework
v0.3.x retrofit (2026-05-20). The `/audit-debt` skill surfaces high-friction
unresolved entries.

- **0** = trivial, reversible (color, UI copy, tuning number)
- **1** = small, locally-reversible
- **2** = moderate; touches one subsystem
- **3** = workaround that ships visibly-wrong-but-shippable
- **4** = architectural, hard to reverse
- **5** = foundational; reversing means rewriting multiple subsystems

## D247 — flat-color-texture-audit: swap flat Lambert surfaces to the EXISTING factories (world-space variants), proven zero-new-programs by a git-stash A/B render (Campaign C39, 2026-06-20)

**When**: M6 unit ③ — fix the ~6–8 weakest flat-shaded surfaces the player sees up close, WITHOUT the D107 real-PBR fork (which the user excluded). The constraint: zero new shader programs, zero asset bytes — reuse the existing 9 procedural factories.

**Scope call (scope-first).** An Explore-agent scan ranked flat `MeshLambertMaterial`/`MeshBasicMaterial` surfaces by player-visibility × cheapness. Shipped 8 surface-groups: fire logs (woodGrain), bedroll pad/pillow/blanket + journal cover/spine + largeTent rug (fabric), journal black-box body + lantern wires (metal), tent + largeTent poles (woodGrain). REJECTED (audit transparency): emissive FX (flame cones, lantern globe, black-box screen — flat-bright is the intent), tiny legibility detail (bandage cross, bedroll fold strips, journal pages — procedural noise would obscure/distract), and distance-only surfaces (tent ropes/stakes, canteen pickups).

**The zero-new-programs mechanism (the load-bearing technical call).** Three.js compiles one program per distinct `onBeforeCompile` source (D207). The audit avoids program creep by reusing factories that ALREADY exist in-scene with the SAME shader-forking options: world-space `createWoodGrainMaterial` (the locker/posts already use it world-space — distinct from the localSpace viewmodel variant), `createFabricMaterial` (the tents), `createMetalMaterial` (the wrecks). The `bark` woodGrain option is a runtime UNIFORM, not a compile-time fork (woodGrainMaterial.ts encodes it as `uBark` per D207), so logs/poles with bark reuse the same world-wood program. `side` (Front/Double) is a material property, not a shader define, so FrontSide fabric (bedroll) shares the DoubleSide tents' program. **PROVEN, not assumed:** a NEW `camp-studio` render (deploy fire/bedroll/lantern + count `renderer.info.programs`) gave 82 programs; a git-stash A/B (stash the 6 material files → re-render flat → 82; restore → 82) confirmed **zero delta**.

**Verification-harness call.** Built `__game.campStudio()` + a `camp-studio` rig scenario as a permanent, reusable gate (deploy the camp objects + report the program count + screenshot for the appearance gate) rather than a throwaway — future material/deploy work can re-run it. The existing scenarios (smoke-plume frames the column from ~12m, item-studio is for held items) couldn't frame deployed world objects up close.

**friction-score:** 1 (mechanical, reversible material swaps onto unchanged geometry, objective-gated by the program count; the only residual is sev-3 cosmetic nits + the journal/tent-pole close-up reads being covered by factory-transitivity rather than a direct render — a walk-test spot-check, not a risk).

## D248 — diegetic survival: per-stat screen-edge vignettes + procedural audio replace the HUD bars, behind a flag + a default-ON player opt-in (Campaign C40, 2026-06-20)

**When**: M6 unit ④ (the M6 finale) — remove the always-on HUD stat bars and let the player FEEL their survival state, the payoff of C38's real survival.

**The gating model.** Two-level: `FEATURES.diegeticSurvival` (compile-time master flag) AND `settings.diegeticSurvival` (a persisted pause-menu opt-in, default true). Effective `diegeticActive = flag && opt-in`. When the flag is off (or the player opts out) the classic bars show — the always-available floor. This honors the proposal's "HUD removal behind a flag + a default-ON opt-in." A tiny shared `ui/diegeticMode.ts` (`isDiegeticActive`/`setDiegeticActive`, set by `menus.applySettings`, read by `hud` + `statVignette`) decouples the readers from `menus` (a circular import otherwise). Flipped the flag ON in this cycle per the AUTONOMOUS flip-authority once the headless + visual gates passed — reversible; the user vetoes FEEL at the Phase-B review. **No save bump** — settings live in their own localStorage key, not the save schema (so this is NOT a D81 stop).

**The tell design.** Every LETHAL stat gets a screen-edge vignette that ramps with severity, extending the WW cold/thirst pattern: cold (blue) + thirst (sepia) were already there (always-on warnings); added heat (amber-orange), hunger (a dark sickly-green starvation pall), and low-health (a deep blood-crimson that PULSES at a heartbeat rate). In diegetic mode the tints BOOST ×1.7 (they're the primary readout, not a subtle warning) so they read over the bright noon desert. Plus procedural audio: a low "lub-dub" heartbeat that quickens as health drops + a stomach growl while starving. The cold/thirst stay subtle in bar-mode (the boost is diegetic-only), so the established bar-mode look is unchanged.

**Two findings worth keeping.** (1) **Hue separation matters for danger states** — the first heat + health passes were both warm-red and the adversarial gate (correctly) FAILed them as indistinguishable in a static frame; fixed by pushing heat to amber-orange and health to blood-crimson (+ the health pulse + heartbeat audio carry the difference in motion). (2) **Render-harness footgun (a CSS-transition vs single-frame-render mismatch):** the vignettes use a 0.4s CSS opacity transition; a headless rig-shot that sets the opacity then screenshots a single frame captures the value MID-TRANSITION (computed opacity ≈ half the target), making the tell look far too faint — the diagnosis (`getComputedStyle().opacity` ≠ `style.opacity`) is the tell. Fix: the render hook disables the transition (`el.style.transition='none'`) so the screenshot shows the true target opacity. Without this I'd have wrongly concluded the vignettes were too weak and over-cranked them.

**Considered alternatives**: (a) always-on tells (heat/hunger/health show even in bar-mode) — rejected (changes the proven bar-mode + the heartbeat is intrusive when you have a health bar); (b) a viewmodel/breath-puff tell for cold — deferred (heavier; the vignette + audio cover it); (c) leave cold/thirst at the WW strength in diegetic mode — rejected (too faint to be the primary readout over the bright desert).

**friction-score:** 2 (reversible flag + settings + CSS/audio, gate-checked by the diegetic-probe; BUT the LOAD-BEARING question — can a player actually survive reading only the tells, or do they need the bars? — is inherently walk-test-only, and the user may veto the whole HUD-removal at the Phase-B review; the heartbeat/growl audio + the always-on-vignette intensity are likely feel re-tunes).

## D249 — procedural-wreck-overhaul: extend the socket-grammar `derelict` (not the legacy ship) with new structure-axis components + a length-frame rig pin (Campaign C41, 2026-06-20)

**When**: M7 unit ⑤ — fix the recurring "every wreck is a long tube" complaint. ASSESS-FIRST (a recon agent) found the answer was already half-built: the ACBA socket grammar's `derelict` archetype builds NON-linear hulls (trimaran/tower/linear), but it was under-varied (3 forms) + under-weighted vs the legacy linear `ship` (which still delegates to the +X-cursor `placeProcgenComposite`, D227 deferred its migration). So most wrecks were still the legacy tube.

**The build call (additive, not a rewrite).** Rather than migrate the legacy ship to sockets (D227's risky deferral), EXTEND the proven `derelict`: 2 NEW components (`splayedEngineCluster`, `dorsalMast`), `assembleDerelict` 3 forms → 5 (every form now carries a secondary feature — the bare-linear branch is GONE so no tube can be the whole ship), `hullBarrel` elongated (a short-fat roll can't read as a blob), and a modest `ARCH_WEIGHTS` shift (~0.08 ship→derelict per biome) so the wider/weirder hulls appear ~as often as tubes. All phash-deterministic (one `seedOf(rand)` draw, everything else hashed) so `verify:placement` + the salvage-panel stream stay stable, and every structural mesh declares a collider (decoration detail exempt via `isWreckDecoration`) so `verify:colliders` passes.

**The load-bearing LEARNING — the end-on framing footgun (a yaw × rig-axis mismatch).** Mid-iteration, the adversarial gate FAILed one seed as a "blob+stick" and I spent a round elongating the hull to fix it — WRONG diagnosis. The real cause: the socket-ship spine is built along local +Z, but the `procgen-wreck` rig framer assumes the subject is X-long (it aims the 3q camera for an X-long ship). `placeProcgenPOI` applies a RANDOM world-yaw, so for some seeds the spine pointed at the camera → the ship was framed END-ON → it read as a blob. A side-angle render proved the "blob" was a perfectly good elongated ship. This is exactly the harness's documented footgun ("a backlit/END-ON shot makes critics grade the camera, not the asset" — `adversarial-visual-critique-harness.md`), but it bites specifically when a SUBJECT'S long axis is randomly oriented relative to a rig that assumes a fixed axis. **Fix:** a `--pinyaw` rig option that pins the spine broadside for a fair gate (default off — normal inspection keeps the real terrain-aligned yaw so ground contact reads true). **The meta-lesson:** before "fixing" an asset the gate flags as malformed, render it from another angle to rule out a framing artifact — the hull-elongation helped but the actual fix was the length-frame.

**Considered alternatives**: (a) migrate the legacy linear ship to the socket grammar — rejected (D227's risk; additive extension of the working derelict is safer); (b) only re-weight (no new forms) — rejected (the existing 3 forms weren't varied enough); (c) keep chasing the "blob" with more hull geometry — rejected once the side-render revealed it was a framing artifact.

**friction-score:** 2 (additive, reversible, gate-passed; the new components are sound; BUT the legacy linear `ship` STILL ships the tube silhouette [now just less often] — a full ship→socket migration remains owed for "no tubes at all", and the in-world FEEL of walking among the new hulls is walk-test-only).

## D250 — the watchtower's ramp-vantage: a WALKABLE inclined collider at a KCC-safe slope, not a ladder movement-mode (Campaign C42, 2026-06-20)

**When**: M7 unit ⑥ (more-wreck-types-new-pois) — the first NEW POI, a scavenged-metal watchtower. The proposal specified "ramp-vantage NOT a ladder": the player should reach the lookout deck by WALKING up a ramp (using the normal KCC movement), not by a special ladder-climb movement-mode (which the project tabled — like the D125 riding-mode spikes, a new movement-mode is a high-friction architectural addition).

**The call.** The watchtower's ramp is a REAL inclined box collider declared at a **~37° slope** (`rampRun = platH * 1.33` → `atan(1/1.33) ≈ 37°`), kept under the KCC ~50° climb limit (`character-controller`) so the normal walk-up just works — no new movement code. The whole structure is ONE `watchtower()` component (legs/deck/ramp declare colliders; braces/railing/roof/posts/rails/mid-strut are `isWreckDecoration` → collider-audit-exempt), placed by a single-component `assembleWatchtower` archetype. This is the FIRST intentionally-WALKABLE procgen POI (prior POIs were obstacles to walk AROUND); the pattern — a ramp/stair as a declared inclined collider under the climb limit — is reusable for future climbable structures (and is collision-honest: the player physically walks the ramp surface, not a teleport/animation).

**Two coupled constraints worth remembering.** (1) The archetype's `list` (crash-lean) is kept TINY (0.04) — a big list would tilt the ramp and could push its effective slope past the KCC limit (un-walkable) or make it read wrong; a built standing structure stays ~level anyway. (2) A new archetype with declared colliders MUST be added to the `collider-audit` default list (`scripts/rig-shot.mjs`, the `collider-audit` scenario) or its coverage is NOT gate-verified — the audit count is the tell (it was stuck at 25 = 5 archetypes × 5 seeds until the watchtower made it 30). This is easy to miss because `verify:placement` passes regardless (it tests the scatter, not per-mesh coverage).

**Scope note**: shipped ⑥ as `[partial]` — the watchtower is one well-iterated POI (2 visual rounds); the well/cistern + debris-trail are deferred to the next cycle rather than rushing three POIs shallowly in one (the campaign's "scope each cycle to ONE unit, ship `[partial]`" guidance).

**friction-score:** 1 (additive archetype on the proven grammar, gate-passed; the only forward note is that the walkable-ramp FEEL — does the ~37° walk-up feel good, can you fall off the open deck — is walk-test-only, and the climb-limit coupling means a future ramp tweak must re-check the slope).

## D251 — a DIRECTIONAL procgen feature needs a strict monotonic gradient on a tight axis + visible 3D mass, NOT a flat ground decal (Campaign C43, 2026-06-20)

**When**: M7 ⑥ — building the `debris_trail` POI (a directional crash-ejecta streak, meant to read distinct from `debris_field`'s scattered disc so a player "follows it to the impact"). Also covers the `well` (a `wellHead` water-cache POI shipped the same cycle) and a reusable `debrisPiece` `scale` param.

**The trail took 3 visual rounds; the lesson is what failed.** r1/r2 both FAILED the adversarial gate as "reads as a scattered PILE, redundant with debris_field." Three coupled causes, each a generalizable footgun for any *directional* procedural feature:
1. **A size gradient only reads if it's STRICTLY MONOTONIC along the axis AND the small end is still visible.** r1 used flat plates (kind 0) near the start — they vanish at a grazing camera angle, so the visible mass was all the big chunks bunched at the impact end → a clump, not a line. Fix: visible 3D masses only (struts/chunks, never flat plates for a feature read end-on), and `scale = min + t*range` keyed strictly to axis position (no big piece near the start, no small piece near the impact).
2. **Lateral jitter is the enemy of a "line" read.** Wide cross-axis scatter (±2m) turned the line into a blob. A directional feature needs a TIGHT centreline (cap offset at ~½ a piece-width) + FEWER pieces with clear GAPS + a LONGER axis so the spread dominates the eye. "Long thin AABB" is the target silhouette.
3. **A flat ground decal is nearly invisible at a grazing FPS/3q angle** — the muted `_scorchMat` (0x564738, barely off-sand) read as nothing. The directional ground cue had to be a much DARKER burn (`_skidMat` 0x241a12) AND the load-bearing cue is really the *debris arrangement*, not the decal. Don't lean a directional read on a flat decal the camera sees edge-on.

The focal mass also has to DOMINATE: the impact chunk is scaled 1.7× (decisively bigger than the largest trailing piece ~1.05×), tilted + bedded deep, so it anchors "the thing that crashed."

**Reusable bit**: `debrisPiece` gained an optional `scale` param that multiplies *every* dimension — geometry, collider half-extents, AND bbox — together, so the collider stays matched (you can't scale via the placement matrix; `transformCollider` assumes rigid). Default `1` → the existing caller (`assembleDebris`) is byte-identical. This is the clean way to add a size gradient to a shared procgen component.

**The well** (same cycle, 2 rounds) reinforced architecture-rule-7-adjacent reads: a "hole" reads as a hole only with a RECESSED shaft (an inner wall into shadow + a near-black unlit void floor), not a dark disc on top (which reads as a lid); a winch reads only with a THICK drum (a thin crossbar reads as a bare gallows); a built structure needs visible MASS + salvaged irregularity (rim-plates), not a thin clean rim.

**friction-score:** 1 (both POIs gate-passed + verify-clean; the forward note is the sev-3 robustness nits — the trail's weakest seed [s7] has a less-dominant impact chunk / fainter crater — and that the directional FEEL is walk-test-only).

## D252 — The solitude principle: POIs must read as century-decayed wreckage, never as recent/maintained human infrastructure (Session C44, campaign)

**Steering (user, 2026-06-20):** *"remove the watchtower… everything in the world should be a wreck/decayed for a hundred years… a watchtower still standing reads as recent and that people could be close by… i want the player to feel alone and have almost no signs of living human life, this goes for pois as well."*

- **Re-scoped the `well` from a maintained water cache to a long-DRY RUIN** — kept the curb + the recessed dry shaft, but COLLAPSED the winch: one leaning broken post, one snapped stub, a fallen cross-beam, slumped rim-plates, scattered rubble. NO rope, NO bucket, NO working crank. (The user chose "keep the well but de-people it" over outright removal.) The curb + the 2 posts still declare colliders.
- **Standing constraint going forward:** every POI and every future structure (incl. M7 ⑦ walkable-wreck-interiors) must read as decayed/abandoned wreckage — NO maintained, currently-used, or recently-built infrastructure. The older POIs (satellite, wrecked_tank, debris_field, husks, derelict, debris_trail) already satisfy this and were left untouched (the user scoped this pass to the two C42/C43 "inhabited-reading" POIs).

**friction-score:** 1 (a clean, reversible removal + re-scope; tsc + the visual gate pass). The forward weight: this is now a **tone INVARIANT** the campaign must hold — a single "someone lives here" prop can undo the solitude the whole world is built for. Also recorded as a vision-delta.

**friction-score:** 2 (the architecture + determinism are sound and gate-verified, but the in-world INTERIOR FEEL — walking in, dark-nav, the dead-console read up close — is walk-test-only; and reusing `dressCrashInterior` couples the ambient wreck to the crash kit, so a future interior-kit change must keep the `aged` path dead).

## D255 — Deep cave ships as ONE chamber; the spec's "4-8 chambers" expansion is deferred to backlog (the funnel bowl can't host a room-spread + descending rooms are walk-test-gated) (Session C51, campaign — M8 ⑨ scope call)

**Context.** ⑨ built the cave over C47-C50: a Sarlacc-style descent funnel → an enclosed roofed chamber (`world/deepCave.ts`, walls + roof + a doorway, declared colliders) → dark-nav (`updateDeepCave`: ambient/sun darken + a no-shadow torch) → decayed dressing (D252). **The constraint found.** The funnel is a radial smoothstep bowl (`CAVE_PIT_CLEARING=22` m, `DEPTH=12` m). Its floor is only ~flat near the centre (r≲4); it rises steeply outward (at r=6 it's ~+2.5 m above centre, at r=9 ~+5 m). So:
- **Adjacent rooms on the bowl floor don't fit** — a chamber offset even ~6 m sits on terrain 2.5-5 m higher than the entry chamber → sub-2 m ceilings or walls buried in the rising bowl. The bowl is too small + too steep for a room-spread.
- **Descending rooms (below the funnel floor) require walking UNDER the terrain heightfield** — and whether the Rapier heightfield blocks the KCC capsule **from below** (vs. letting it pass) is an unresolved architectural question (D254's open risk). Building multi-level under-terrain rooms blind, with no way to walk-test the KCC handoff, risks shipping broken traversal (player stuck at the surface or falling through).

**Decision.** Ship ⑨ as a **complete single-chamber cave** (descent funnel + roofed enclosed dark dressed chamber + the ⑩-egg-site dais) — it's verified + delivers the M8 "deep cave" experience. **Defer the 4-8-chamber expansion to [backlog.md](backlog.md) §A**, gated on a WALK-TEST that resolves the under-terrain-heightfield KCC question (does the player walk down a ramp under the terrain sheet, or get blocked?). If the walk-test confirms it, the expansion is an `addChamber`-helper + descending ramps; if not, it needs an architecture revisit (a bigger carve, or the trimesh approach rejected in D254). The user reviews this at the Phase-B milestone (after M10) with real play feedback — the right place to decide whether to invest in the expansion.

**friction-score:** 2 (a scope reduction from the spec, surfaced honestly rather than built-blind; the single chamber is sound + the expansion is genuinely blocked on a walk-test, so deferring beats shipping broken multi-level traversal — but it IS less than the GDD's cave ambition, to be reconsidered at the milestone review).
