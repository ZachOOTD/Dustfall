# Research: Escape-pod cylindrical / capsule forms

**Researched**: 2026-06-28  
**Trigger**: User rejected industrial modular box (candidate B); wants cylindrical pod instead.  
**Depth**: medium

## Summary

Real reentry capsules use 1.8–2.3 meter length-to-diameter ratios (Vostok sphere ~1:1, Mercury truncated cone, Soyuz 2.1 m × 2.2 m "headlight"). A cylindrical lifeboat reads most authentic when vertical (standing capsule, parachute/antenna on top, heat-shield base scorched) or horizontal (tube-in-sand, like a wreck). Five distinct directions anchor on real engineering (Soyuz proportions, Mercury heat-shield, Vostok sphere + rivets) and retro-industrial (riveted aluminum tube, Airstream inheritance). Vertical orientation is stronger for "waking beside it + salvaging" — the heat-shield base is a natural salvage point, and the silhouette is more striking half-buried.

## Key findings

1. **Vostok sphere = 1:1 length-to-diameter ratio (2.3 m diameter, 2.4 m overall)** — pure sphere, aluminum alloy + titanium bulkhead, spherical because it rotates uncontrolled in reentry and needs 360° heat protection. Strong differentiation from boxy pods; reads mysterious. Source: [Vostok (spacecraft) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_(spacecraft))

2. **Soyuz descent module = 2.1 m × 2.2 m, "headlight" hybrid** — a hemispherical upper section + shallow 7° cone + spherical heat-shield base. Balances aerodynamic lift (not a pure sphere) with reentry stability. 3-person volume; for one person, could compress proportions. Source: [Soyuz spacecraft](https://www.russianspaceweb.com/soyuz_sa.html)

3. **Mercury capsule = truncated cone with ablative heat shield** — cone shape (not cylinder), parachutes at narrow end, retro-pack solid rockets attached to heat shield base. 19.2 m main parachute, landing bag deployed from heat shield. Readable as reentry hardware. Source: [Project Mercury - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Mercury)

4. **Airstream aluminum riveted cylinder** — ~3,000 rivets per trailer, streamlined cylinder shape for aerodynamics, lightweight aluminum skin. Since 1936, the iconic "silver bullet" silhouette. Reads cheap, domestic, DIY-salvageable. Source: [Airstream Travel Trailers | Iconic Design](https://www.airstream.com/travel-trailers/)

5. **Vertical vs. Horizontal orientation:** real descent modules land vertical (parachute recoil, center of mass gravity); horizontal orientation (tube-in-dune) is cinematic but implies a crash tumble or debris field. Vertical reads as "the pod ejected successfully and deployed; it's *meant* to stand upright." Source: [Reentry capsule - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reentry_capsule)

6. **Single-occupant capsule volume ≈ 4–6 m³ internally** — Soyuz descent module is 4 m³ for 3 crew; one pilot needs ~2 m³ working room + systems. External diameter 2.0–2.3 m is believable. Source: [Spacecraft - Soyuz](https://www.russianspaceweb.com/soyuz_sa.html)

## Five cylindrical pod directions (all vertical-standing)

### 1. **Soyuz-inspired "Headlight Bell"**
- **Proportions:** hemispherical bulge (upper 1.1–1.2 m) → 7° tapered cone (0.6 m) → flat or slightly domed heat-shield base (0.3 m deep).
- **Surface:** radial seams from apex, ribbed thermal tile panels on cone section, riveted equatorial band, offset thruster/antenna nubs, asymmetric scorch on base.
- **Viewport:** small (20–25 cm) recessed circular porthole on the upper hemisphere, offset slightly forward.
- **Material language:** polished aluminum + scorched titanium base; reads serious reentry engineering.
- **Salvage points:** heat-shield base (bolt-on), cone-mounted thruster block, thermal panels.
- **Why it fits:** immediately recognizable as *real* descent hardware, not a generic sci-fi trope. Proportions proven in orbit (Soyuz flies today). Half-buried with the dome tilted, it reads *grand* and mysterious.

### 2. **Vostok Pure Sphere + Heavy Rivets**
- **Proportions:** 2.3 m diameter perfect sphere with a shallow 0.3 m flat base (heat shield).
- **Surface:** dense latitude/longitude riveted seams (200+ visible rivets), large dent clusters from micrometeorite + reentry pitting, random thermal-tile patches, antenna stub on apex, thruster block on side.
- **Viewport:** small (18–22 cm) circular porthole, deeply inset (recessed by 0.15 m), pilot's eye-level offset.
- **Material language:** weathered aluminum alloy + hand-patched titanium base; industrial/Soviet aesthetic.
- **Salvage points:** antenna/thruster modules, porthole frame, base-mounted cargo hatch, removable thermal panels.
- **Why it fits:** dense, passive, alien-but-real. A sphere is *underused* in games — immediate differentiation. Half-buried in a dune, only the top hemisphere visible, it reads like an artifact. Low-poly sphere naturally subdivides for surface detail.

### 3. **Mercury-Style Truncated Cone + Retro Heat Shield**
- **Proportions:** ~1.8 m long, tapered from 2.0 m base diameter to 0.8 m nose, 30° cone angle.
- **Surface:** ribbed/paneled external frame (bands of exposed girders + cable tray), conical heat-shield base (0.4 m deep), scorched/ablated surface texture on base, band-deployed parachute container recessed on top.
- **Viewport:** small (20 cm) circular window on the forward-facing cone (not nose tip—offset below apex so it doesn't catch impact).
- **Material language:** bare steel framework + heat-blackened base; looks like a re-entry experiment or Mercury-era capsule.
- **Salvage points:** heat shield (whole bolt-on unit), parachute container frame, thruster block, external cable trays, window frame.
- **Why it fits:** reads explicitly as mid-century reentry hardware; the taper makes it unmistakably *not* ODST. The exposed frame structure signals "modular" + "strippable." Less symmetrical than Soyuz, more weathered-looking.

### 4. **Riveted Aluminum Torpedo / Airstream Lifeboat**
- **Proportions:** 2.0 m long, 0.9 m diameter cylinder, rounded hemispherical nose (0.5 m deep) + flat or slightly domed stern (0.2 m base).
- **Surface:** hand-riveted aluminum sheets (visible seam rows), dented + patina'd, minimal exterior hardware (just antenna stub + thruster nozzle on side). Frost/condensation haze on interior of porthole.
- **Viewport:** large oval/teardrop nose window (0.6 m tall × 0.4 m wide), unframed transparent acrylic, thick-walled. Off-center by ~0.2 m to avoid nose impact.
- **Material language:** brushed/dulled aluminum, no paint; looks *cheap* and practical (independent hauler's tool).
- **Salvage points:** all exterior panels (hand-riveted, easy to unbolt), nose cone (can be detached), thruster module, window frame.
- **Why it fits:** lightweight, simple, *scrappy* — Firefly/Expanse indie vibe. Aluminum rivets are iconic + low-poly-natural. Half-buried with nose tilted up, it reads playful + mysterious. The large window is a compromise: not claustrophobic in first-person, but small enough to feel tight.

### 5. **Hybrid: Soyuz Bell + Nostromo Panel Language**
- **Proportions:** Soyuz-style headlight (hemispherical + cone + flat base), but the cone section wraps in *exposed rectangular paneling* — like the industrial modular box (B) but in cylindrical form.
- **Surface:** polished aluminum hemisphere (top 60%) → welded horizontal panel bands on the tapered cone (0.5 m each, with bolt-line seams), scorched heat-shield base, asymmetric cargo-hatch door recessed on one side of the cone.
- **Viewport:** small recessed porthole on the upper hemisphere, plus a separate secondary rectangular window on the cargo-hatch panel (peephole for hatch status).
- **Material language:** aluminum dome + steel panel exoskeleton; looks like "expedition pod meets industrial shelter."
- **Salvage points:** heat shield, thruster block, cargo-hatch door (entire panel), porthole frame, removable panels from cone section.
- **Why it fits:** explicitly reads as *modular* + *salvageable* (the panel language is Nostromo DNA). The Soyuz dome gives visual drama + reentry credibility; the panels are *strippable* (perfect for the tutorial hint: "panels can be removed for salvage"). Tight middle ground between "real capsule" and "obviously game-salvage-able."

## Orientation: Vertical is stronger

**Vertical (standing):**
- Heat-shield base as a natural visual anchor + salvage point (bolts are visible, radiated tiles can be peeled).
- Parachute/antenna on top reads as "successful landing" — dramatic when half-buried with the top tilted.
- Player's first view: wake *beside* the pod, crane neck up to look at it — innate drama.
- Entry/exit is at the dome or side hatch — natural climbing/extraction narrative.

**Horizontal (tube-in-sand):**
- Reads as a *crash* (tumbled on reentry, came down sideways) — signals distress + mystery.
- Silhouette half-buried is striking but suggests debris-field + wreckage (less "I need to salvage this" vibe).
- Harder to justify ladder/hatch access from inside (player would have to climb a tilted tube).
- Less likely in procedural generation (vertical is the landing-state; horizontal requires a secondary flip event).

**Verdict:** vertical standing is on-tone for "lone pilot ejects successfully, reentry works, pod lands + buries partway → you wake beside it." If the narrative *requires* a crash-tumble, horizontal works, but vertical is cleaner.

## Half-buried silhouette drama

These shapes read most powerfully **half-buried + tilted:**
1. **Vostok sphere** — only the top dome visible above sand, the tilted edge catching dawn light. *Mysterious artifact.*
2. **Soyuz bell** — the bulbous top emerging, the cone swept by wind + sand. *Gravity + elegance.*
3. **Torpedo cylinder** — the rounded nose pointing upward, the rear half buried. *Sleek wreck.*

The truncated cone (Mercury style) is less dramatic half-buried because the shape reads *better* from the side — bury it vertically and you lose the taper.

## Actionable takeaways

For the procedural-modeler rebuild:
- **Pick one direction** (recommend Soyuz headlight #1 or Vostok sphere #2 for visual drama; hybrid #5 if salvage-clarity is essential).
- **Lock proportions:** use real capsule L:D ratios (Soyuz 2.1 m × 2.2 m, Vostok 2.3 m sphere, Torpedo 2.0 m × 0.9 m).
- **Vertical orientation** — stack the model from base (heat shield) → cone/cylinder → dome apex.
- **Viewport discipline:** keep it 18–25 cm diameter, offset from center, recessed inward by 0.15–0.2 m. Use it as the first salvage hint: players crawl up to peer in.
- **Surface language:** radial or horizontal seams (not random paneling), rivets clustered near seams, dents/pitting asymmetric.
- **Heat shield base:** scorched (dark radiated pattern), with visible bolt holes or thruster attachment brackets — reads as a *strippable module*.
- **Low-poly strategy:** subdivide the sphere/dome only where seams/rivets gather; keep the smooth sections smooth. Use baked normal maps for rivet detail rather than geometry.
- **Material:** weathered aluminum (slight bloom, matte + specular variation), titanium heat-shield base (darker, scorch texture), copper/brass thruster nozzles (verdigris/patina).

## Contrarian or surprising

- **Spheres are underused in game design** — players expect capsules to be streamlined or conical. A perfect sphere (Vostok) immediately reads as "not a generic ODST clone" and **signals that this is real spacecraft hardware**, not fantasy. Risk: it's also slightly abstracted (less obviously "salvageable" at a glance). Mitigation: heavy rivets + panel patches make it read as industrial + modular despite the spherical form.

- **Soyuz's 7° cone is *barely* tapered** — it doesn't "look" like a cone at first glance. The hemispherical top dominates. This is precisely why it works for reentry (aerodynamic lift + stability) but might require *seeing the model from the side* to read the full "headlight" shape. In an isometric or top-down overview, it reads as a dome; in a first-person approach, the cone becomes clear.

- **Horizontal orientation is cinematically tempting but misleading** — a pod that lands horizontal implies a catastrophic crash (or a deliberate spaceflight that burns sideways, like a shuttle). Real capsule design assumes vertical landing. If the narrative is "you bailed out successfully," vertical is correct and **stronger visually** (the dome is the first thing you see emerging from sand).
