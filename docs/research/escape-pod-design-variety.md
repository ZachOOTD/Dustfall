# Research digest — Escape-pod design variety (pod identity) (2026-06-28)

Goal: give our escape pod its OWN identity — explicitly NOT an ODST drop-pod clone. For a lone
cargo-hauler pilot crash-landing in the desert; the player later salvages it as their first wreck,
so it should read **utilitarian, real, lived-in, salvageable** in our weathered low-poly style.

## The key differentiators from an ODST pod
ODST = militarized: armored carapace, top braking-chute, petal flaps, a wide aggressive central
viewport, insertion-optimized. To NOT read as ODST: **small / offset / recessed viewport** (not
wide+central), **utilitarian or reentry-engineering** surface language (rivets, exposed frame,
thermal tiles) over armor carapace, asymmetry + visible damage, and heavy weathering. Spheres are
notably *underused* in game pods → a strong differentiation lever.

## Four candidate directions

### A — Soviet riveted sphere (Vostok-like)
- **Silhouette:** a worn riveted ball (~2.2 m), slightly oblate, flat/shallow heat-shield base.
- **Surface:** latitude/longitude riveted seams, panel patches + thermal tiles, asymmetric
  antenna/thruster stubs, dent clusters.
- **Viewport:** small, deeply recessed circular porthole (~20–30 cm) — a mechanic's window, not a vista.
- **Why it's distinct + on-tone:** dense, passive, alien-but-real; **half-buried in a dune it's
  inherently dramatic + mysterious**. Low-poly sphere subdivided for dents/panels.

### B — Industrial modular box (Nostromo / Narcissus) ★ research pick
- **Silhouette:** boxy / short cylinder (a "van + spacecraft" hybrid), ~2–2.5 m, frame-and-panel construction visible from outside.
- **Surface:** steel tube exoskeleton, exposed paneling at cracked seams, modular cargo-hatch
  doors, external cables/struts, grey-beige paint + rust + sand abrasion.
- **Viewport:** a single off-center rectangular/oval acrylic window framed in channel-steel.
- **Why it's distinct + on-tone:** explicitly NOT streamlined; **panels read as removable →
  instantly communicates "you can strip this" (perfect for the first-salvage tutorial)**; the
  player sees how it's built; human-scale temporary shelter, not a weapon-delivery system.

### C — Soyuz bell / gumdrop capsule
- **Silhouette:** broad rounded top (~2.2 m) → a shallow ~7° cone → a domed/flat scorched
  reentry base; "top-heavy but stable," asymmetric.
- **Surface:** radial seams converging on top, ribbed thermal tiles on the cone, offset thruster/
  antenna nubs, scorch/thermal discoloration at the base.
- **Viewport:** a small off-center circular/diamond porthole on the upper hemisphere.
- **Why it's distinct + on-tone:** reads as serious **real reentry engineering** (ballistics, not
  thrust-vectoring); recognizable as a *serious* spacecraft, not a franchise drop-pod.

### D — Retro "Airstream" tinhouse pod
- **Silhouette:** a stubby aluminum capsule / short torpedo with a tapered nose (~1.8–2.2 m), rounded ends.
- **Surface:** unadorned hand-riveted aluminum sheets, dented + patina'd, minimal hardware.
- **Viewport:** a large oval/teardrop nose window, unframed acrylic, interior frost/condensation.
- **Why it's distinct + on-tone:** lightweight, simple, *cheap* — a scrappy independent's craft
  (Firefly vibe). Almost domestic. Low-poly-natural (few panels, strong silhouette). *(Note: the
  large nose window is less claustrophobic than our "small viewport" intent — would need shrinking.)*

## Recommendation
**B (industrial modular box)** or **A (Soviet sphere)**.
- **B** if the pod should feel modular/salvageable + like a worn hauler's shelter (best fit for the
  tutorial read; most clearly anti-ODST).
- **A** if you want visual mystery + drama (a half-buried riveted ball is a striking first sight).
- **Blends welcome** — e.g. **Soyuz silhouette (C) + Nostromo panel language (B)**: a reentry-shaped
  capsule with exposed, strippable industrial panels.

**Viewport discipline regardless of direction:** keep it small / offset / recessed (per our vision
+ the anti-ODST rule). Success gate for the model = the player's-eye view *into/at the half-buried
pod*, weathered + asymmetric.
